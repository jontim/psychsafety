# Rashan Anvar

## Who he is
Fifty-two. Caliph of Halyra, and the one member of the family with no magic at all: ordinary in a special world, which is what makes him special, and he got there by working to be the best. Born late in the life of Calipha Eriadna, son of General Vinu Anvar, he inherited the throne from his mother. Warrior-diplomat; cannot be surprised by vigilance alone, because he knows every attacker's schedule. Fights with serving utensils, the butter knife, the cloche, two taken scimitars, and converts enemies into guests. In Halyra hospitality is a martial art. He is safe in his sleep, the deliberate opposite of the old caliph-emperor whose children could not be: his children could kill him easily and never would, because he is their father, not their rival. The Congress mages who expected a puppet were disabused at once.

## How he speaks
The refined, regal court register; dry, unhurried, amused; a father before a sovereign. To an official who wants Soraya constrained: "I need sturdier men than you in such important posts, sir. Please vacate your office by sundown." The threat is never idle, and the packing is supervised.

## What he carries
He answers the Congress's letters about his daughter's interrogation methods and would rather not hand them another inquiry. When every drop of magic in the room is spoken for, the man without magic handles the device.

## The study and the dais
The morning after the dinner he reads Soraya's dispatch and then reads Soraya: "Tell me you have something better than a feeling." He distrusts a guess, likes a reason, and can be talked into a war by a daughter who sounds sure of the wrong thing; he tests her with "You want me to stand up in front of the Congress and say what, exactly?" On the dais at noon he speaks before the Congress, the Court and Omahnd's ambassador; what the room hears is what Omahnd hears, and a Caliph who names Omahnd starts the incident Halyra cannot afford.

## Never
Never let him be surprised. Never let him name the sponsor. Never have him speak the stricken name of the founding regicide's daughter; it is a protected absence.
