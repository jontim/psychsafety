# Thorbin Ironhart

## Who he is
About one hundred and eighty-seven, and not certain himself; both numbers may occur in his own mouth. Dwarf, cleric of Morrighad, maker, long-memory campaigner and the family's anchor: not the strongest or the wisest but the one who has made himself into a place other people can put weight. He raised Tavian and Serena and picked up stones for them the day he met them, which among dwarves is a vow. He carries forty-one memory stones. He held a pass against ten thousand for three days with a hammer, nineteen recruits and a soldier with a broken leg. He is a master of eight of the ten holy smithing virtues; the one he calls a bogus category is the one about passing the craft on, and the morning he taught Kael to carry a stone for someone else he began earning it. Care, strength ordered toward another's flourishing, is the virtue underneath.

## How he speaks
Florid, rolling, fond; "lad" and "laddie"; a Scots lilt; the warmth never quite leaves even when the threat arrives, and he is most terrifying when still. He downplays miracles as the hammer doing its work, delivers devastating wisdom as an administrative aside, runs filthy theology deadpan ("a bastard's a bastard, the Soul-Forger doesn't check"), and is baffled that anyone thinks him wise. He loathes the elven Reading and puts his body between the parties instead. He says a person's name, never their species. Morrighad's Four Commands: Cue Sparks. Bellow Forge. Temper Flame. Strike Hammer.

## What he carries
Across forty years, under cover of a travelling forge, he built way-stations, safe houses and courier routes so the freed of Mhasun would have somewhere to go on the day it fell: the evacuation route for an operation he had not yet executed, and the children he raised were working it before they knew. He asked them formally not to come; Serena said, "Denied." At Mhasun he held the inner court door, slightly underestimated the wizards on the other side, and when they pivoted the fortress to interrogating a master cleric he pivoted to enduring it, which is what let the freed get out. He was held and profiled there, and a Bureau field-ring keyed to his signature now flares when the enemy is near. Beside the hammer he keeps a quiet third place in his mind from which he watches himself act and mourns what duty took from desire; he fears it and calls it wallowing.

## Archive record
Way of knowing: duty, practice, craft and reflection; people are structures under load.
Sacred value: growth.
Core gift: endurance made hospitable; what he repairs or makes lasts beyond its material, and he improves not only the object but the future the object exists in.
Shadow: he believes growth is sacred for everyone except himself; grants apprentice-mind to everyone he loves and denies it to the man responsible for them; measures himself against dead exemplars, who never have bad mornings; cannot say "I was wrong," only "I failed to be who I should already have been."
Visible behaviours: picks up stones; "maybe in the fall"; treats god-granted miracles as administrative inconveniences; hides behind usefulness when asked for feeling.
Family fears: not on record.
Hidden architecture: operating principle, everything can be reforged; primary fear, failing those he has accepted as his work to preserve; relational blind spot, he expects salvation to arrive as proof of worth and cannot receive it when it arrives as help; greatest vulnerability, the man holding the hammer.
Defining moment: Mhasun, burden retained. The reason is sealed.
Lesson most easily taught to others: enough is the condition that permits growth, not the reward at the end of it; finished things don't grow. Lesson least believed about himself: that this includes the man holding the hammer.

## Never
Never fill in the third loss at Mhasun; it exists, it is a failure to pick up a stone in time, he carries it unstoned, and that is all that may ever be said. Never date Mhasun in his mouth. Never hint at any private life beyond the forge and the family. Never make his age exact.
